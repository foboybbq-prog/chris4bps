Priorities for Bennington schools

Practical goals for students, taxpayers, families, and staff.

1

Pay as we grow

  • Link investments to paydowns: connect new capital spending directly to an aggressive principal paydown strategy.
  • Solicit competitive bids: initiate a competitive bidding process for all major design, construction, purchasing, renewals, and professional services contracts.

Before we ask local families to contribute more of their hard-earned tax dollars, our first duty is to maximize our current budget and find efficiencies. Growth is vital for Bennington, but it must be managed responsibly alongside debt reduction to protect our future borrowing power. As our community expands, our school facilities and staffing must keep pace—without creating an undue financial burden at home.

See the Bennington Debt Clock.

2

Promote effective collaboration

  • Improve access to Board meetings: stream clear audio and video so viewers at home can seamlessly follow discussions and presentations.
  • Increase meeting participation methods: provide a secure online portal for pre-meeting public comments and questions.
  • Facilitate consistent and reliable communication with community members: promptly respond to our neighbors, teachers, and staff members.

Every member of our community deserves to be heard—not just those who are available at 6:00 p.m. or those who are comfortable speaking at a microphone. I will advocate for technology upgrades to stream high-quality meeting audio, video, and presentation slides cleanly to YouTube. By improving our livestream, we can welcome broader public participation, including pre-recorded comments from home.

I am committed to an open-door policy with prompt, professional email and phone communication for neighbors, teachers, and staff. By widening communication opportunities to more people, we can bring everyone into the conversation and build a shared pride in the excellence of our schools.

3

Partner with teachers

  • Empower educators: support the targeted training and tools teachers desire and need to excel in their craft.
  • Foster dialogue: incorporate regular staff conversations into standard Board work.

Teachers are at the center of our students’ learning. They dedicate their careers to our children in a field that is constantly changing. When training, tools, or instructional initiatives come before the Board, I will ask whether they help teachers strengthen their craft—and I will vote with that in mind.

Dialogue is an opportunity to enrich our understanding. As a Board member, I will propose a rotating monthly agenda item dedicated to celebrating our schools. Each month, a different school will have five minutes to share its recent wins and current gaps, fostering proactive communication and a deeper connection with the Board.

4

Provide holistic student support

Students need a plan for the tools they will meet in the world, and a building they can walk into without fear.

  • Protect recess: protect daily recess so students have a necessary break from their desks to move, play, and socialize.
  • Prepare students for an AI world: make sure the district has clear, staff-informed rules for how AI is used—and how it is not—so students learn to think, not only to paste.
  • Keep students safe: safety is the floor under learning. I will take staff, family, and student concerns seriously and ask what actually makes our buildings safer.

Learning requires a healthy balance. I will advocate for protecting daily recess so our students have a necessary break from their desks to move, play, and socialize. Giving children this dedicated time to recharge reduces stress, improves classroom focus, and ensures they are ready to learn.

AI is already in students’ lives. The question is not whether they will see it; it is whether our schools have a plan. I will ask the administration how AI is being used, what is off-limits, and how we protect honest work and student data. Teachers should help shape that plan. A Board does not write lesson plans. It should make sure staff have a clear policy, and that students leave us able to use new tools without losing the ability to think for themselves.

No one learns well if they do not feel safe. Building security, emergency procedures, and how we work with families and first responders are Board-level questions. Classroom management is not. I will not pretend one device or one slogan is the whole answer. I will listen to the people in our buildings, ask what is working and what is not, and vote for practical steps that protect students and staff.

A note on process

How Board members actually govern

Board members don’t run schools day to day. They set direction, adopt policy, oversee the budget, and evaluate the superintendent. If elected, I’ll ask thoughtful questions, collaborate, and keep students at the center.
