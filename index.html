Why I’m running

Bennington schools are our community’s future

Our children and our community deserve a school board member who listens first, carefully examines the status quo, asks difficult questions, and keeps students at the center of every decision. Living in Bennington since 2006, raising a BPS graduate, and regularly attending School Board meetings, have taught me a great deal about our schools and how they operate. In addition, my life and work experiences have prepared me to strengthen the Board by keeping oversight consistent and practical, bringing community members to the table, and helping Bennington schools maintain their standard of excellence while operating in a fiscally responsible manner and effectively and efficiently supporting our students, teachers, and staff.

My Priorities

Partner with teachers

Great teachers change lives in a way screens can’t — ensure teacher pay is a priority, analyze school budget and real needs in the classroom, and protect recess time and human connection.

  • Empower educators: provide the targeted training and tools teachers desire and need to excel in their craft.
  • Foster dialogue: proactively schedule regular conversations with staff.

Teachers are at the center of our students’ learning. These professionals dedicate their careers to our children in an ever-evolving field. To maintain our schools’ legacy of academic excellence, I believe it is important to empower our teachers to strengthen their crafts, which benefits both our teachers and our students.

Too often I have seen conversations with teachers and staff occur only when a Board agenda item has become urgent. I will encourage staff engagement as a partnership and matter of practice by scheduling regular listening sessions.

Provide holistic student support

Next step, not just next test.

  • Increase partnerships: expand collaborations with Metro Community College and other local organizations.
  • Protect recess: support our students’ physical health just as passionately as we support their academic growth.

Some of our students thrive within and are prepared for internships, apprenticeships and jobs. Therefore, I will focus on increasing the number of local partnerships we have to facilitate this path to readiness for students.

Education shouldn’t stop at the desk; I will advocate for policies that strengthen both the minds and the bodies of our youth. By focusing on both the minds and bodies of our students, we can ensure students stay healthy, focused, and ready to learn.

Pay as we grow

Everyone pays. Public, private, and home-school families all carry BPS spending. Spend wisely. Work the debt down.

  • Prioritize existing efficiencies: evaluate possible spending reductions, resource consolidations, and strategic project delays.
  • Link investments to paydowns: connect new capital spending directly to an aggressive principal paydown strategy.
  • Solicit competitive bids: initiate a competitive bidding process for all major design, construction, purchasing, renewals, and professional services contracts.
  • Reserve funds purposefully: evaluate whether utilizing a portion of our large cash reserves to retire high-interest debt would optimize our long-term funding for future projects.

Before we ask local families to contribute more of their hard-earned tax dollars, our first duty is to maximize our current budget and find efficiencies. Growth is vital for Bennington, but it must be managed responsibly alongside debt reduction to protect our future borrowing power. As our community expands, our school facilities and staffing must keep pace—without creating an undue financial burden at home. Therefore, we must focus our attention on:

Existing Obligations: Bennington has $198,575,000 in outstanding bond principal as of September 2026 (BPS Budget Hearing Presentation).

New Infrastructure: The voter-authorized $112 million High School #2 bond (2025) is already included in that outstanding figure—not added again on top. About $12.6 million ($12,645,829) in district reserves is going toward the HS#2 project (total project cost $124,645,829).

Current Tax Demand: The proposed 2026–27 combined school levy is $1.345 per $100 of taxable valuation—not yet adopted (board vote Wednesday, September 23, 2026, 5:00 PM). The prior adopted combined levy was $1.358. For a home with $300,000 of taxable value, the school portion at $1.345 would be about $4,035 a year.

Historical Trend: Estimated interest on current debt is about $6,350,431 a year (~$0.20 per second, ~3.20%). That is a campaign estimate, not a district-published continuous rate. The 2025 HS#2 bond was presented with a 0.0¢ tax-rate increase; long-term debt is still paid through the bond levy. See Bennington Debt Clock.

Promote effective collaboration

  • Improve access to Board meetings: Send audio and video digitally to the YouTube stream so viewers at home can seamlessly hear every discussion and view slide presentations.
  • Increase meeting participation methods: create a secure online submission portal that allows community members who cannot attend in person, or who prefer not to speak at a microphone, to submit questions and comments before meetings.
  • Facilitate consistent and reliable communication with community members: promptly respond to our neighbors, teachers, and staff members via email and phone.

I believe that it is vital to invite everyone to the table and to create a space in which participation can happen and voices can be heard. Therefore, I will advocate for digitally transmitting what is already being said into the microphones in the room today, and digitally transmitting the PowerPoint slides, so people watching from home get the same experience as those who attend in person—if not a better one. I will work with the school on technology solutions so audio and video reach the YouTube stream cleanly, without new ceiling microphones.

I am committed to establishing a responsive, open-door policy by ensuring prompt, professional responses to our neighbors, teachers, and school staff members via email and phone. This engagement in productive conversations has a primary goal: shared pride in the excellence of our schools.
