Why I’m running

Bennington schools are our community’s future

Our children and our community deserve a school board member who listens first, carefully examines the status quo, asks difficult questions, and keeps students at the center of every decision. Living in Bennington since 2006, raising a BPS graduate, and regularly attending School Board meetings, have taught me a great deal about our schools and how they operate. In addition, my life and work experiences have prepared me to strengthen the Board by keeping oversight consistent and practical, bringing community members to the table, and helping Bennington schools maintain their standard of excellence while operating in a fiscally responsible manner and effectively and efficiently supporting our students, teachers, and staff.

My Priorities

Pay as we grow

Before we add spending, use what we already have well: pair new capital with paying down principal, bid major contracts, and find efficiencies.

  • Link investments to paydowns: connect new capital spending directly to an aggressive principal paydown strategy.
  • Solicit competitive bids: initiate a competitive bidding process for all major design, construction, purchasing, renewals, and professional services contracts.
  • Prioritize existing efficiencies: evaluate possible spending reductions, resource consolidations, and strategic project delays.

Before we ask local families to contribute more of their hard-earned tax dollars, our first duty is to maximize our current budget and find efficiencies. Growth is vital for Bennington, but it must be managed responsibly alongside debt reduction to protect our future borrowing power. As our community expands, our school facilities and staffing must keep pace—without creating an undue financial burden at home. Therefore, we must focus our attention on:

Paydowns: Bennington has $198,575,000 in outstanding bond principal as of September 2026 (BPS Budget Hearing Presentation). The school board needs to step up and insist on clear debt reduction strategies before approving any new investment.

Solicit competitive bids: Major design, construction, purchasing, renewals, and professional services should go out so more than one qualified firm can compete. Open bidding is how we keep the cost of building down and give every capable contractor a fair shot—so the district is not locked into a single relationship when the community is paying the bill. See Bennington Debt Clock.

Promote effective collaboration

  • Improve access to Board meetings: Send audio and video digitally to the YouTube stream so viewers at home can seamlessly hear every discussion and view slide presentations.
  • Increase meeting participation methods: create a secure online submission portal that allows community members who cannot attend in person, or who prefer not to speak at a microphone, to submit questions and comments before meetings.
  • Facilitate consistent and reliable communication with community members: promptly respond to our neighbors, teachers, and staff members via email and phone.

I believe that it is vital to invite everyone to the table and to create a space in which participation can happen and voices can be heard. Therefore, I will advocate for digitally transmitting what is already being said into the microphones in the room today, and digitally transmitting the PowerPoint slides, so people watching from home get the same experience as those who attend in person—if not a better one. I will work with the school on technology solutions so audio and video reach the YouTube stream cleanly.

School Board meetings begin at 6 p.m. There are technology tweaks that could easily be implemented to bring more voices to the meeting. When you provide a good-quality livestream, you build a larger audience and open the opportunity to play pre-recorded videos from the community with their comments and questions. If we want to hear from a larger base, we need to grow beyond hearing only from those who are comfortable speaking in public and only from those who can be on site at 6 p.m.

I am committed to establishing a responsive, open-door policy by ensuring prompt, professional responses to our neighbors, teachers, and school staff members via email and phone. This engagement in productive conversations has a primary goal: shared pride in the excellence of our schools.

Partner with teachers

Great teachers change lives. I will listen to teachers, staff, and the community, and I will vote for initiatives that give teachers the training and tools they need and that make staff input a regular part of Board work.

  • Empower educators: support the targeted training and tools teachers desire and need to excel in their craft.
  • Foster dialogue: make staff conversations a regular part of Board work, not only when something is already urgent.

Teachers are at the center of our students’ learning. They dedicate their careers to our children in a field that keeps changing. When training, tools, or instructional initiatives come before the Board, I will ask whether they help teachers strengthen their craft—and I will vote with that in mind. That benefits teachers and students both.

Too often staff are heard only when a Board agenda item is already urgent. I will show up, listen to school presenters and the community, and expect the Board to hear from staff as a matter of practice—not only in a crisis.

Provide holistic student support

Students need more than a test score: a path after high school, and a school day that supports their physical health as well as their academic growth.

  • Protect recess: support our students’ physical health just as passionately as we support their academic growth.
  • Increase partnerships: expand collaborations with Metro Community College and other local organizations.

Many students who misbehave or didn’t finish an assignment are handed a punishment of no recess. While taking away privileges is often looked on as a viable method of correction, I don’t feel recess should be treated as a privilege. Recess should be mandatory. I will advocate for policies that strengthen both the minds and the bodies of our youth, ensuring students get their exercise, are focused, and ready to learn.

Students need advisors they can actually get to—someone who can help them map high school before senior year is already here. Colleges and scholarships look at more than ACT or SAT scores. They look at what a student built, led, or learned outside class: a job, an internship, a business, volunteer hours. Those essays and deadlines often come due long before graduation. College is expensive, and a student put on the right path early has a better shot at aid and at a school that fits. Our counselors cannot carry that load alone. I will support partnerships with colleges and other local organizations that can help students plan, apply, and compete for that aid.
